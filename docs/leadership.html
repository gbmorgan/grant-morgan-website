Leadership

Research Leadership

My leadership is centered on building the systems, incentives, and research capacity that help people and institutions accomplish ambitious individual and collective goals.

Across administrative and scholarly roles, I approach leadership through systems thinking and evidentiary reasoning. Decisions do not operate in isolation. Policies, incentives, resource allocations, and organizational structures create both intended and unintended consequences.

Research-Enterprise Leadership

As Associate Dean for Research, I work to strengthen the research enterprise through infrastructure, incentives, mentoring, and systems that support externally funded scholarship.

Infrastructure

Building Research Capacity

Development of structures and staffing that help faculty identify opportunities, develop proposals, manage awards, and sustain externally supported research.

This work has included the development of a dedicated research-support office and expanded research-administration capacity.

Incentives

Aligning Effort and Research Support

Creation of policies that connect externally funded effort to resources that faculty can reinvest in their research.

These systems support activities such as:

  • graduate research assistance;
  • research travel;
  • pilot studies;
  • scholarly dissemination;
  • faculty research accounts;
  • new research development.

Workload

Connecting Grants and Faculty Work

Development of processes that more directly connect grant-supported effort with teaching workload and course-buyout decisions.

The goal is to make faculty workload, grant budgeting, effort allocation, and institutional support more coherent and transparent.

Mentoring

Developing Faculty Research Programs

Mentoring junior and mid-career faculty as they clarify research priorities, articulate coherent programs of scholarship, identify funding pathways, and communicate the significance of their work.

Leadership Philosophy

Systems Thinking and Evidentiary Reasoning

Policies, incentives, and organizational decisions should be evaluated as parts of systems rather than as isolated actions.

Effective research leadership requires attention to:

  • evidence;
  • incentives;
  • resource constraints;
  • intended outcomes;
  • unintended consequences;
  • institutional culture;
  • long-term sustainability.

The central question is not simply whether a decision solves an immediate problem. It is whether the decision strengthens the broader system in which people conduct research, allocate effort, and pursue individual and collective goals.

Developing Strong Research Programs

I work with scholars to strengthen five features of a successful research program.

01

Coherence

How do individual studies, collaborations, and funding activities connect to form a recognizable scholarly program?

02

Impact

Why does the work matter, and what intellectual, practical, methodological, or social contribution can it make?

03

Rigor

Are the research design, measurement, methods, evidence, and interpretations sufficiently strong?

04

Intellectual Leadership

What questions, ideas, frameworks, or directions does the scholar lead rather than merely participate in?

05

Progress

Is the research program developing cumulatively through visible outputs, stronger questions, broader influence, and increasing independence?

Scholarly and Collaborative Leadership

I frequently contribute to interdisciplinary work as a methodologist, statistician, evaluator, collaborator, reviewer, and advisor.

My role is to provide methodological expertise that allows substantive experts to focus on the constructs, theories, and questions they know best.

Effective interdisciplinary research depends on:

  • clear division of expertise;
  • respect for substantive and methodological roles;
  • early methodological involvement;
  • shared responsibility for evidentiary quality;
  • communication across disciplinary boundaries.

Selected Leadership Impact

Growth in Research Capacity

During my tenure in research leadership, the Moody School of Education has experienced substantial growth in:

  • research expenditures;
  • externally funded faculty participation;
  • proposal activity;
  • research infrastructure;
  • faculty research support;
  • reinvestment of salary savings into research activity.

Research expenditures increased by approximately 1,800% during this period.

This figure reflects organizational growth that occurred during my leadership and should not be interpreted as the work of any one individual. Research expansion depends on faculty expertise, administrative support, institutional investment, collaboration, and sustained collective effort.
